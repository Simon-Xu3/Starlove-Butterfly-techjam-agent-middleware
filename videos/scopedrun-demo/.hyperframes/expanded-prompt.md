# SLB-ScopedRun — expanded production prompt

## Style block

Build a 1920×1080, approximately 170-second narrated technical demo using the
exact design tokens in `frame.md`: canvas `#07110F`, surfaces `#101C19` and
`#172622`, ink `#F4F7F5`, muted ink `#9BAEA7`, hairlines `#294139`, allow
`#2FC8A0`, deny `#FF5C57`, and admission `#F2B84B`. Use Oswald 700 for display
statements and IBM Plex Mono 400/700 for evidence, captions, and operational
metadata. The mood is cinematic security evidence, not a generic cyber HUD.

## Rhythm declaration

`HOOK → orient → BUILD → prove → DENY → turn → PROVE → hold/close`

The peak is Frame 7, where model-independent namespace evidence resolves the
security claim. Frame 5 and Frame 8 deliberately hold still long enough for
denial and limitation language to read.

## Global rules

- Treat the mount boundary as a physical aperture and reuse its vertical line,
  clipped routes, and registration marks across scenes.
- Maintain three depth layers: sparse grid/aperture shadow in back, content or
  footage in midground, and status metadata/hairlines in foreground.
- Reveal on narration cues across the full shot; never dump the frame in the
  first quarter.
- Use long-tail `power3` arrivals, velocity-matched internal seams, and held
  reads. No bounce, infinite motion, lazy breathing, or late camera drift.
- Reserve the bottom 17% for captions.
- Crop real product footage to one readable claim at a time; preserve the real
  interface and avoid rebuilding it.
- Default transition is a 0.4-second blur crossfade. Use a single zoom-through
  when architecture becomes the live product. Evidence sequences may hard-cut.
- BGM is sparse, precise, and subordinate to speech. Use quiet click/lock marks
  on explicit Delegation, allow, deny, and revoke; remove the bed under the
  first limitation sentence of the close.

## Frame 1 — The boundary that prompts cannot provide

**Concept:** Prompt fragments and a hidden picker appear as flimsy layers an
Agent with a shell can see around. A single hard aperture line draws through
them and turns the scene from suggestion into enforcement.

**Mood:** Noir title sequence meets an operating-system security diagram.

**Depth:** BG perspective boundary grid and clipped ghost paths; MG prompt and
picker fragments; FG oversized thesis and `BOUNDARY / 01` metadata.

**Choreography:** Fragments type on and are crossed out mechanically; the
boundary SVG draws; “PROMPTS ASK” lands first, then “MOUNTS ENFORCE” locks in
with the allow color. Hold the final line without ambient drift.

**Transition:** Blur crossfade as the boundary line extends into Frame 2's
trusted-sequence route.

## Frame 2 — Standing permission is not Run scope

**Concept:** Travel across one wide trusted-sequence world. Entitlement defines
the outer map; Delegation narrows it; admission gates it; the Runner receives
only the compiled read-only route.

**Mood:** Precision transit map, but every station is real domain language.

**Depth:** BG sparse coordinate grid; MG route and aperture; FG generation,
ownership, path, and runtime check labels.

**Choreography:** Camera pans Entitlement → Delegation → Re-check. Each check
draws only when spoken. All unused resource branches sever and fade before the
read-only mount passes through. Receipt stages lock in last.

**Transition:** The final mount rectangle grows toward camera and becomes the
real browser surface in Frame 3.

## Frame 3 — Explicitly delegate Inventory

**Concept:** The real UI is the proof surface. The edit distinguishes Advisor
suggestion from the human's explicit selection using two separate punch-ins.

**Mood:** Calm product documentary; no simulated cursor or fake chrome.

**Depth:** BG canvas and aperture shadow; MG real browser clip; FG two concise
labels, `SUGGESTION ≠ AUTHORIZATION` and `ONE RUN`.

**Choreography:** Browser enters at readable scale. Punch into Advisor result,
hold, then slide focus to the Resource selector as Inventory is chosen. A small
allow line draws around the selected Run scope; unrelated options dim.

**Transition:** Push-slide into the submitted Run.

## Frame 4 — Allow and proof chain

**Concept:** The Run visibly progresses from human choice to server decision to
Runner evidence. The successful Agent answer proves delegated files were usable;
namespace absence is reserved for Frame 7. The chain is built from the product's
own stages rather than a generic checklist.

**Mood:** Incident response control room, confident and readable.

**Depth:** BG route traces; MG real Agent output and proof panel; FG stage index,
resource ID, generation, and `RUNNER STARTED` label.

**Choreography:** Running state holds briefly. The completed answer appears,
then an editorial zoom lands on Delegated. Decided and Executed lock in on their
spoken phrases. The three-stage trail remains fully readable for the hold.

**Transition:** Mechanical squeeze into a split evidence frame.

## Frame 5 — Deny before Runner

**Concept:** Guidance and enforcement sit side by side. The left proves the
Advisor reveals no Payments metadata; the right proves direct API bypass still
fails admission.

**Mood:** Cross-examination evidence board, not an error-screen montage.

**Depth:** BG severed route grid; MG browser and terminal plates; FG denial code,
`RUNNER STARTED: NO`, and two synchronized evidence labels.

**Choreography:** Left plate seats first. Right plate opens like the other half
of a dossier. `HTTP 403` decodes, then `entitlement_missing` lands in red. A
supporting HTTP-gate card then records Runner call count zero. Hold the complete
denial for at least two seconds.

**Transition:** Denial red line snaps to amber and becomes the entitlement state
rail in Frame 6.

## Frame 6 — Revoke and retry

**Concept:** A permission state flips while history remains intact. The future
route closes; the earlier allow receipt stays behind the gate as an immutable
record.

**Mood:** Mechanical circuit breaker with a visible audit ledger.

**Depth:** BG historical allow receipt; MG active/revoked entitlement card; FG
generation and prospective-revocation note.

**Choreography:** Active status changes to revoked. The next-run route draws
toward the Runner, hits the closed aperture, and retracts. The earlier allow
receipt sharpens behind it and holds.

**Transition:** Vertical push from decision evidence into terminal test proof.

## Frame 7 — Namespace proof

**Concept:** Traverse a real test log, then pivot into a clean evidence matrix.
The visual climax is not a log line—it is the simultaneous truth of readable,
immutable, rejected, absent, and unchanged.

**Mood:** Forensic test lab with documentary restraint.

**Depth:** BG hash fragments and fixture manifest; MG real terminal capture;
FG three-column evidence matrix and matching before/after hashes.

**Choreography:** Camera follows only the safe passing assertions. The terminal
slides aside; the evidence matrix assembles sequentially with allow and deny
colors used semantically. Matching hashes draw together and lock.

**Transition:** Blur to near-silence and the limitation anchor.

## Frame 8 — Honest close

**Concept:** A real filtered request first proves that a Capsule cannot silently
fall back to `local-process`: `runtime_profile_unsupported` lands before Runner
invocation. The remaining limitations then clear into the final lockup.

**Mood:** Confident engineering review; candor is the premium signal.

**Depth:** BG nearly empty aperture shadow; MG real terminal proof and fixed
limitations; FG small next-step rail and final product line.

**Choreography:** The terminal proof seats first and holds on HTTP 403 plus
`runtime_profile_unsupported`. Remaining limitations replace one another by
hard mechanical cuts. Everything clears except the aperture and the final three
fragments: “ONE RESOURCE. ONE RUN. NOTHING ELSE.” Hold the last frame in silence.

## Recurring motifs

- One vertical aperture line that opens only for an allowed Resource.
- Run/Receipt coordinates in the upper-left and lower-right corners.
- Resource routes that are physically absent after denial, never merely dimmed.
- Status colors used only as facts: teal allow, red deny, amber admission.
- A small `SLB / SCOPEDRUN` wordmark treatment assembled from type, not a
  fabricated logo.

## Negative prompt

Avoid generic hackers, padlock icons, shield stock art, matrix rain, random
code, neon purple-blue gradients, glass cards, fake dashboards, fake browser
chrome, invented product metrics, unbounded particle systems, constant zoom,
bouncy easing, unreadable terminal output, or any implication of hot
revocation, tenant authentication, or general RBAC.
